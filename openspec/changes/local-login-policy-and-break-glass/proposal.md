## Why

Phase 2 gave the dashboard real accounts, real providers and a real step-up, and 2c-2 gave a company its group-to-role rules — but the local password form is still open to everybody on every install, and there is still exactly one shape of emergency access: an ad-hoc branch in `dependencies.py` that serves *any* password-verified cookie behind the proxy. A company that has moved its people to the reverse proxy cannot close that door, and the moment it could, closing it would be the most dangerous button in the product: the setting that keeps the identity provider honest is also the setting that locks the install out when the identity provider is down. PLAN.md §4.6 answers both halves at once — `local_login_policy` closes the door, the qualifying break-glass invariant of §4.2 makes sure it can always be opened, and the four host commands of §4.6 are the way back when even that fails. This change ships all three together because shipping any one of them alone is either useless or unsafe.

## What Changes

- **The policy**: `dashboard_settings.local_login_policy` = `enabled` (default) | `admins_only` | `break_glass_only`, one Alembic revision on the current single head, **no environment variable, no `Settings` field, no tier entry** (`.env.example` stays 47 lines, `[settings_fields]` stays 96). It joins `SECURITY_SETTINGS_FIELDS`, so `security:write` and step-up apply for free, and it replaces the ad-hoc password-fallback branch in `app/core/auth/dependencies.py` — under the default `enabled` the replacement is a no-op, so nobody who can sign in today stops being able to.
- **Two-stage break-glass**: `is_break_glass` stays a *designation* that an admin may carry without a second factor (the migration backfilled exactly that on the compat `admin`). A **qualifying** account is `is_break_glass AND status=active AND role = admin preset AND totp_secret_encrypted IS NOT NULL AND password_hash IS NOT NULL`, computed on read, never stored — the password is one of the facts because a proxy-provisioned admin with no local credential cannot use the local form the whole invariant exists to keep open. Every gate counts qualifying accounts; nothing counts designations, and `break_glass_only` admits a qualifying account rather than a bare designation, so the strictest policy is never weaker than `admins_only`.
- **One guard, eight call sites**: a single post-state service function refuses with `409 last_break_glass_protected` any change whose *resulting* state would leave zero qualifying accounts while the policy is not `enabled`. Called from the role PATCH, the status PATCH, `DELETE` user, clearing `isBreakGlass` (a new field on the update request), self-service `/totp/disable`, self-service `DELETE /password`, admin `reset-totp`, the new shared `deactivate_user()` back-channel (refusal audits `scim_deprovision_refused`), and custom-role delete-and-reassign when it ships. Mapping re-evaluation is exempt by construction — break-glass accounts are `role_source=manual`. Like the last-admin rule, the guard is serialised and re-applied inside the write — the write intent is taken before the path's first read (SQLite's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` is a no-op once a transaction is open) and nothing commits between the count and the write.
- **Two gates in the other direction**: tightening the policy away from `enabled`, and enabling a provider that offers no local password fallback, both need at least one qualifying account and otherwise answer `409 break_glass_requires_totp` **naming the account**, so the refusal is also the instruction. Both hold the account write intent across the count and their own commit, so a concurrent account mutation cannot slip between them. Relaxing and disabling are never gated. `PATCH /api/auth-providers/{id}` therefore starts accepting `enabled` (it answers `422` today) and audits `provider_enabled`/`provider_disabled`.
- **Sign-in**: a break-glass account always completes a second factor whatever the TOTP toggles say, emits `break_glass_login` at `critical` severity (the codebase's first critical audit rows), and mints a session marked with an optional `bg` claim on the unchanged version-2 payload. A policy refusal is byte-identical to `401 invalid_credentials` and audits `login_failed reason=login_policy`.
- **Rate limiting**: two buckets, both spent by every attempt, of which only the per-account one is cleared by a success — a per-(client address, normalized username) budget of 8/60 s, so one address can no longer bar a username everywhere, **plus** the coarse per-address ceiling of 60/60 s that is the endpoint's actual cap. The per-account bucket is local by construction, so on its own an address could mint a fresh bucket for every username it invents and spend a blocking password-hash comparison on each. **No account is exempt**: both keys carry the client address, so an attacker cannot bar the operator signing in from elsewhere, and an exemption would tell an unauthenticated caller which account is the emergency one. The coarse bucket is deliberately *not* cleared by a success: an attacker holding one valid account would otherwise reset the endpoint's only ceiling whenever they liked. Recorded attempts are aged out by the periodic cleanup scheduler's leader pass, because only a successful sign-in of that same username clears them.
- **Frontend**: `login.local_login` and `login.providers` widen from placeholders to real values; the local form is collapsed behind a link under `admins_only` and reachable only at `/login?local=1` under `break_glass_only` (a public route that also redirects a signed-in session to the dashboard instead of 404ing); the pending-identity screen honours the same rule so it cannot become a second door; the Organisation group gains a third child, the login-policy card, which shows the policy, the qualifying state, the "turn on two-factor for `<admin>`" hint and the emergency URL and account name to save in a password manager — and which renders even with no reverse-proxy row, because local sign-in exists on every install. The header gains an "Emergency session" indicator. The username is never sent to an unauthenticated client.
- **Recovery**: four `codex-lb admin` commands — `reset-password <username>`, `local-login enable`, `disable-provider <id>`, `reset-login-policy` — acting on the database directly, taking no environment variable, making no network call, never importing the app, each auditing `break_glass_cli_used` at `critical` severity with `auth_method=cli`. `reset-password` also re-opens local sign-in when its own write would leave nobody the policy admits (the `--clear-two-factor` case under `break_glass_only`), because a recovery command that can strand the operator is not one. `docs/sso.md` is the runbook, registered in the mkdocs navigation, and finally writes down the 2b side effect: behind a reverse proxy an admin must enrol two-factor before it can manage people.

Not in this change: the OIDC provider, its three-step wizard and the test-login pre-flight (Phase 3a); the SCIM endpoints, whose `active=false` path is the reason `deactivate_user()` exists (3b); audit webhooks (Phase 4); the custom role editor and its delete-and-reassign transaction, whose call site is specified here and implemented in Phase 5b; API key ownership columns (Phase 4).

## Capabilities

### Modified Capabilities

- `admin-auth`: the policy setting, the qualifying predicate, policy-gated password sign-in and header fallback, always-two-factor and critical audit for break-glass, the emergency session marker, the two failed-login buckets and their retention sweep, the tightening gate, and the four host recovery commands.
- `dashboard-users`: the single bidirectional guard and its call sites, `isBreakGlass` on the update request, and the shared `deactivate_user()` back-channel.
- `identity-providers`: `enabled` becomes writable on the provider PATCH and is gated on a qualifying break-glass account.
- `frontend-architecture`: the policy-driven local form, `/login` as a public route, the login-policy card as the Organisation group's third child, the pending screen reconciliation, and the emergency-session indicator.
- `user-documentation`: `docs/sso.md`, the recovery runbook.
