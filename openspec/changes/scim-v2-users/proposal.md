## Why

PR-3a connected a company identity provider, so a person who already has an account can sign in with it. Nobody can yet be *given* an account by the identity provider, and — the half that actually matters to a security team — nobody can be taken away by one. Today an administrator invites every joiner by hand and remembers to disable every leaver by hand. The published failure of that arrangement is not that it is slow; it is that the leaver is the one who gets forgotten, and their API keys keep spending money for as long as nobody notices.

Everything the deprovisioning half needs is already on main and has no caller. `deactivate_user(user_id, *, actor, actor_ip, source)` was written for this change: it takes the accounts write intent before its first read, runs the qualifying break-glass post-state predicate, cascades the owned keys with `deactivated_reason='owner_disabled'`, bumps `session_generation`, and already audits `scim_deprovision_refused` at WARNING when the predicate refuses. `AuditAuthMethod.SCIM` and `DashboardUserRoleSource.SCIM` already exist. `access_summary.scim_tokens` is already on the wire, already read by the disclosure tier, and hard-coded to `0`. This change is mostly the act of connecting things that were built pointing at it.

The two failures this is written against are both about a provisioning path handing out authority nobody asked it to. LiteLLM re-enabled keys an administrator had deliberately revoked, because "restore the user" was implemented as "undo everything"; the reason column that prevents it is already in this schema and this change is its first machine-driven caller. Sentry's SCIM integration accepted a role value it did not recognise and quietly applied a default — the identity provider's operator believed they were provisioning what they typed, and were not. Here an unrecognised role is a refusal that names the value, and so is a *recognised* one the provisioning path may not hand out; silently defaulting down is the same defect as silently defaulting up, because both leave the person at the identity provider believing something false.

## What Changes

- **`/scim/v2/Users`**, a router of its own outside `/api/`, with its own bearer dependency, its own error envelope and no dashboard principal anywhere in its dependency tree: create, read, replace, patch, and list with `userName eq` filtering. **No `DELETE`** — identity providers soft-disable, so `DELETE /scim/v2/Users/{id}` answers `405` naming the supported form, and a deprovisioned account stays rediscoverable by `userName eq` because deactivation never removes the identity row.
- **`dashboard_scim_tokens`**: label, `token_hash` (unique sha256 digest), non-secret `token_prefix` for display, `provider_key`, `last_used_at`, `rotated_at`. Issue and rotate return the plaintext exactly once and nothing else ever does. Rotation is in place, so the label, the id and the last-sync history survive it and the previous secret dies the moment it commits. One migration parented on the current single head.
- **`active: false` runs `deactivate_user()`**, whole cascade included, and MUST pass the qualifying break-glass predicate — a change that would leave zero qualifying accounts answers SCIM `409` and audits `scim_deprovision_refused` exactly once, from inside the shared function, which is why the route must not audit it a second time. `active: true` runs a new principal-free twin that flips the status, commits, and only then restores keys whose reason is `owner_disabled`; a key an administrator revoked by hand carries `manual` and is never resurrected. The enable direction is bounded by the same predicate as the role push: an account holding a role this surface does not hand out — an administrator a human promoted and then disabled — answers `409` and stays off, because switching it on would restore both the account and the keys its disable cascaded. A push that carries attribute changes alongside `active` commits them in the lifecycle function's own transaction, so a refused deprovision takes the whole request back instead of leaving the rename applied.
- **A live invite for a deprovisioned account is deleted while the account row stays.** No primitive did that: revoking an invite deletes the whole account, which would take the row `userName eq` has to keep finding. The `invited` case is closed the same way — the invite goes, the row moves to `disabled` — because today `deactivate_user` returns `False` for an invited account and an `sso_only` invite never expires, so an identity provider deprovisioning a not-yet-accepted joiner is a silent no-op that leaves a live sign-up link behind.
- **A pushed `roles` slug is normalised, looked up, and bounded.** Unknown → `400` naming it. Recognised but not assignable (`guest`, and `member` until Phase 4) → `400` naming it, with a reason that distinguishes it from a typo. Recognised, assignable, and admin-level → `400`, because a provisioning path may not create or promote to an admin; §4.2 and the delegation rules are worked through in `design.md`. Every refusal audits. The account's role is left alone entirely when a human has pinned it to `role_source=manual`, and equally when the account already holds a role this surface would not itself hand out — a role a bearer token may not grant is one it may not take away — with the skip audited once per account rather than on every scheduled re-push. `PUT` and `PATCH` reach all of this through one parser: a patch is folded into the attributes of a user resource and validated by the model that reads a replace body, so neither verb can have a length cap, a `primary` flag or the one-role rule that the other lacks.
- **Identities are `provider=scim` rows** whose `subject` is `externalId`, written through the resolver's own slug rules rather than a second copy of them: `slugify_subject`, the `-2`/`-3` collision walk, the reserved `admin` name, the three-attempt `IntegrityError` retry, and the "e-mail already taken stays on the identity row" rule. Accounts are never resolved by username, so a `userName` that collides with a local account derives a new name and can never take one over.
- **`/scim/v2` is exempt from the CSRF middleware twice over** — it is outside the `/api/` prefix the middleware inspects and it is always bearer-authenticated — so this change adds a regression test and a requirement rather than a middleware branch. It does add `scim/` to the SPA fallback's excluded prefixes, without which `GET /scim/v2/Groups` answers `index.html` with `200` and an identity provider reads that as "groups are supported".
- **The automatic account management card**, last in the Organisation group, after the login-policy card: the base URL to paste into the identity provider, issue and rotate showing the secret once, the last sync, and revoke. It is disabled, with the reason stated, until a company sign-in provider is enabled — not as decoration but because a SCIM-provisioned account has no local password and no way in until one is.
- **`access_summary.scim_tokens` stops being `0`.** Without that one count the card this change adds is undiscoverable the moment the group collapses, and the enterprise disclosure tier never fires.

**A correction this change is obliged to make.** The requirement `Accounts are deactivated through one shared function` asserts that in this release the callers of `deactivate_user()` are the user PATCH's status change and the identity resolver's demotion path. Neither has ever been true in code: `update_user` inlines `deactivate_owned_keys` and `update_role_status_guarded`, and `IdentityResolver._demote` sets `status = disabled` directly with no owned-key cascade at all, so a mapping demotion to disabled leaves the account's keys live. This change makes the sentence true of SCIM and rewrites the rest of it to say what the code does, naming the resolver's cascade gap as the open defect it is. Routing those two call sites through the shared function is a second concern with its own regression surface and is not in this change.

Not in this change: `/scim/v2/Groups` and the mapping re-evaluation a group push drives, and OIDC back-channel logout (PR-3b-2). `/scim/v2/ServiceProviderConfig` goes with them, because a discovery document written now would either advertise group support that does not exist or be rewritten one change later. Audit webhooks and the audit-export card are Phase 5a. Nothing here changes the OIDC provider, the role-mappings card, the invite API, or the CSRF middleware.

## Capabilities

### Added Capabilities

- `scim-provisioning`: the `/scim/v2/Users` protocol surface, `dashboard_scim_tokens` and their one-shot plaintext, what a SCIM token may and may not authenticate, the shared deactivation and its break-glass refusal, the owner-cascade reactivation, the bounded role slug, the identity rows and the username collision rule, the machine-to-machine properties (CSRF, rate limits, bounded body), the error envelope, and the access-summary count.

### Modified Capabilities

- `dashboard-users`: `Accounts are deactivated through one shared function` — the SCIM caller, the `invited` case, the twin that reactivates, the refusal naming the invariant that actually refused, and the correction above.
- `identity-providers`: `Just-in-time usernames` — the empty-slug fallback prefix becomes the provisioning provider's rather than the trusted header's, and the rules are stated as belonging to every provisioning path.
- `frontend-architecture`: `Organisation settings group` — the automatic account management card joins the mount order, last.
