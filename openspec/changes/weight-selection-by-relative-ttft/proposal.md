# Weight Fresh Weighted Selection by Fleet-Relative First-Token Latency and Per-Model Output Throughput

## Why

The balancer has no latency signal. The weighted strategies (`capacity_weighted`, `relative_availability`) draw by remaining credits times the error-rate multiplier; everything else that shapes a fresh pick is a hard filter (health tier, routing policy, earlier-reset bucket, quota-planner cost, overload backoff, in-flight pressure). On the production pool a cohort of accounts answers the same small, low-effort prompts noticeably later than its siblings (first-token p50 2.4-2.9 s versus 1.7-1.9 s, driven by upstream reasoning on ~40% of their turns) yet receives its full credit-proportional share of fresh bindings, and every conversation that lands there pays the slower cohort for its whole sticky lifetime.

First-token latency is not the whole picture. Production evidence from 2026-09-09 (24 h of Codex traffic, rows with more than 200 output tokens) shows that on `gpt-5.6-sol` the fleet splits into two **output-throughput** cohorts -- 9 accounts at a p50 of 39-40 tok/s against 10 accounts at 66-75 tok/s (one at 108) -- while their first-token latency is the same (7.0-8.0 s at 60k+ cached input). On `gpt-6-astra` the same accounts are uniform (44-56 tok/s). The dominant per-account speed difference is therefore **model-specific output throughput**: a property of the (account, model) pair that a per-account first-token signal cannot see, and that a per-account throughput signal would misattribute (an account slow on sol would be discounted on astra too).

The only operator recourse today is `routing_policy=preserve` on the slow accounts: a hard exclusion that idles their capacity, cliff-switches traffic once the fast accounts exhaust, goes stale as cohort membership drifts, and cannot express "slow on this model only".

## What Changes

- **Replica-local TTFT samples (per account).** The request-log funnel records the first-token latency of successful `normal` turns with fewer than 20 000 uncached input tokens (input minus cached prefix, so long sticky sessions keep sampling), reasoning effort absent/`minimal`/`low`, no response-create-gate or bridge-queue wait, and a single upstream send with no account-capacity wait, into a bounded per-account ring (1 h window, 64 samples). The sample is measured from the upstream `response.create` send, not from request-state creation: the row's `latency_first_token_ms` starts at the request state, and on the bridge path that precedes session-lease reacquisition, optional prewarming, image inlining and payload slimming, of which the bridge-queue wait captures only the final gate; the finalizer therefore passes the start-to-send offset (`response_create_sent_at - started_at`) to the funnel as `latency_upstream_send_ms` and the sampler records `latency_first_token_ms - latency_upstream_send_ms` (the HTTP stream re-anchors its attempt clock right before the send and passes `0`). A row without a send anchor is not sampled (fail closed). `warmup`, `compaction` and `realtime_live` rows, error rows, queued rows and WebSocket/bridge rows that retried `response.create` or waited for account capacity are never sampled: their first-token latency is measured from the request start, so it would contain the failed attempt plus the recovery sleep and, after an account switch, be charged to the destination account. Nothing is persisted.
- **Replica-local throughput samples (per account and model).** The same funnel records, for successful `normal` turns with a model, at least 200 output tokens, a first token and a positive generation span, a single send and no queue wait -- on any input size and reasoning effort -- the output throughput `output_tokens / (span to the upstream terminal event - latency_first_token_ms)` into a bounded ring per (account, model) (same 1 h window and 64-sample cap; models left without in-window samples are dropped). `output_tokens` is the upstream's total, reasoning tokens included; see Known Limitations for where the reasoning lands relative to the span and why the total is still the numerator. Rows without a model (direct WebSocket rows carry `""`) or with the `unknown` placeholder add nothing. The 200-token floor keeps the quantization of `tokens / duration` small. The span ends when the upstream terminal frame is parsed, not when the row is written: the WebSocket/bridge finalizer awaits the API-key settlement before it computes the row's `latency_ms`, so a 400-token turn generated in 10 s followed by a 10 s settlement would otherwise read 20 tok/s instead of 40 and discount a healthy account against HTTP or unkeyed traffic; the HTTP stream yields the terminal frame and writes its row only in the generator's `finally`, so a slow downstream client or an upstream connection that lingers after `response.completed` would inflate the span the same way; and the bridge consumes upstream events through its own reader, which performs durable alias, operation, recovery and circuit-settlement writes before the finalizer runs. Every path therefore stamps the terminal at its parse site -- the direct WebSocket reader and the bridge reader set `upstream_terminal_at` on the matched turn, the HTTP stream sets `upstream_terminal_at` on its `_StreamSettlement` before yielding the frame -- and hands the funnel `latency_upstream_terminal_ms`, which the sampler uses as the span's end.
- **Two fleet-relative soft weights.** First token: when at least 3 accounts hold at least 8 samples, each account's upper-trimmed mean (slowest decile dropped) is compared with the fleet median of those estimates; an account slower than the fleet by more than 15% gets `max(0.5, fleet / account)`. Throughput: for the model the selection is for, when at least 3 accounts hold at least 8 samples **for that model**, each account's median throughput is compared with the fleet median of the per-account estimates **for that model**; an account below the fleet by more than 15% gets `max(0.5, account / fleet)`. Evidence on other models never contributes, and a build without a requested model (quota planner) leaves throughput neutral.
- **Combination by minimum.** The draw weight is multiplied by `min(ttft_multiplier, tps_multiplier)`, never their product: both signals measure the same defect (a slower serving path) from two angles, so compounding would punish one cause twice and breach the floor either signal promises. The combined multiplier compounds with the error-rate multiplier and is consulted only by the weighted draws: sticky owners, hard continuity, deterministic probes and deterministic strategies are unaffected, and no account is ever excluded.
- **Model-aware state build.** `select_account` already receives the requested model; it is now threaded through `UnboundSelectionRequest` / `StickySelectionRequest` and the opportunistic-admission request into `_prepare_sticky_selection_states(model=...)` and `_build_states(model=...)`, so the multiplier is computed for the model the draw is for.
- **Transition log.** One INFO line `latency_cohort_weight_change` when either signal's multiplier crosses 1.0 or moves by more than 0.1, carrying the combined multiplier, the signal that set it (`ttft`, `tps`, `both`, `none`), the model, which signal transitioned (`changed=ttft|tps|both`), both per-signal multipliers and estimates, sample and pool counts, and no account identifiers (the state build has no redaction flag). Each signal is gated at its own scope: the first-token multiplier per account (`RuntimeState.ttft_weight`), so one TTFT transition is one line however many models are requested; the throughput multiplier per (account, model) (`RuntimeState.tps_weights`), so alternating requests for a model where the account is slow and one where it is not do not flap the log. Only non-neutral throughput weights are stored, so the per-model map is bounded by the models the account is currently discounted on, not by the model strings clients send.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `account-routing`: weighted strategies discount relatively slow first-token latency and relatively slow per-model output throughput, combined by minimum; the replica-local transient-signal list gains recent first-token latency samples and per-model throughput samples.

## Impact

- New `app/modules/proxy/_load_balancer/ttft_cohort.py` (first-token constants, sampler, estimator, fleet reference), `throughput_cohort.py` (throughput constants, sampler, median estimator, per-model fleet reference) and `latency_cohort.py` (minimum combination, transition log, `apply_latency_cohort_weights`). `RuntimeState` gains `ttft_samples`, `tps_samples` (model -> samples), `ttft_weight` (last applied first-token multiplier) and `tps_weights` (model -> last applied non-neutral throughput multiplier); the detached opportunistic snapshot copies all of them. `_write_request_log` calls both samplers after its Prometheus phase observations and takes `latency_upstream_send_ms` (not persisted; the first-token sample's start, `None` drops the row) and `latency_upstream_terminal_ms` (not persisted; the throughput span's end); `_WebSocketRequestState.upstream_terminal_at` carries the direct-WebSocket and bridge readers' terminal stamp to the finalizer, which also derives the send offset from `response_create_sent_at`; `_StreamSettlement.upstream_terminal_at` carries the HTTP stream's stamp (set by `streaming.helpers._stamp_terminal` at both terminal-detection sites of `_stream_once`, read by `_upstream_terminal_latency_ms` in its request-log call; `streaming/mixin.py` stays at 1100/1100). `_build_states` applies the combined weight after building states and takes the requested model; `_prepare_sticky_selection_states` and the three selection paths thread it (`load_balancer.py` at 3009 after `_prepare_sticky_selection_states` became a delegator to `sticky_selection.prepare_selection_states`; ceiling 3021. `select_account` at 490, ceiling 527).
- Zero-config: no new `CODEX_LB_*` setting, no schema, migration, API or dashboard change; Settings stays at the 130 ratchet. Both signals are fleet-relative and floored, so they are neutral on thin evidence and on a uniformly slow fleet; that is why no toggle is shipped. The error-rate precedent's toggle exists because that discount can reach 0.05; these never drop below 0.5 combined.
- Behavior change for weighted strategies only: the fast cohort's fresh share rises from its credit share to at most about 1.6x that share relative to a floored account; on the observed sol fleet the slow cohort is drawn at about `40 / 66 = 0.6` of its credit weight while astra draws stay unchanged. Established owners never move because of it.
- Accounts on other paths: HTTP-upstream rows never carry gate/bridge wait values and measure both latencies per attempt, so the queued-wait and retry filters only drop WebSocket/bridge rows that actually waited or retried locally. The WebSocket finalizer passes a new `upstream_retried` funnel argument (`response_create_attempt_count > 1`, `replay_count > 0` or an account-capacity wait was started); it is not persisted and keeps the row out of both samplers.
- Observe-only opportunistic admission builds states on a detached runtime snapshot; that build applies the same multiplier but does not emit the transition log (`log_weight_transitions=False`), because the weight written to the snapshot is discarded and the next live build would repeat the line.

## Known Limitations

- Replica-local by design; each replica converges on its own samples (existing requirement, now enumerating both signals).
- Sample volume: an account needs 8 eligible turns per hour (per model, for throughput) and 3 accounts need evidence; quiet hours and rarely requested models are neutral by design. If production shows a weight stays neutral, widening a window or a filter is a module-constant change, not a setting.
- `prefer_earlier_reset` still narrows to the earliest-reset bucket first; the weights act within that bucket.
- The first-token slice has no model dimension: an account whose API key or model catalog steers it a heavier model mix can be discounted for its mix rather than its health. The uncached-input and effort filters bound this; the throughput signal is per model precisely because the production evidence showed the model dimension matters there.
- Throughput samples use the total `output_tokens`, reasoning included, while where the reasoning lands depends on the client: with reasoning summaries requested the first summary delta is the first token, the reasoning runs inside the span and the total is what the span produced; without summaries the whole reasoning prefix sits inside the first-token latency and the sample overstates the span's throughput by `total / visible` -- several-fold on a turn that reasons for longer than it answers, not a small bias. No numerator is exact for both kinds of turn (visible tokens alone would under-read every summary-streaming turn by the same factor). The total is kept because the comparison is fleet-relative: fresh draws spread clients across accounts, so the summary/no-summary mix is shared and the per-account medians move together; a per-account median only shifts when more than half of that account's in-window samples are inflated, and the fleet reference only when more than half of the accounts' medians are, so a spurious discount needs an uneven mix that persists over most of an hour on most of the fleet. Inflation reads fast, so it never discounts the account it lands on. If production shows such a skew, the request-log funnel would need a per-row "summaries streamed" flag to pick the numerator per turn; that is a follow-up, not a setting.
- No Prometheus gauge in this change; the transition log and `request_logs` are sufficient to judge whether the weights move. A gauge is a follow-up once they do.
